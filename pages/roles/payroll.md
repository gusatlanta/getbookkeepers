## What a payroll specialist owns

- **Each payroll run.** Collecting hours, approving time, entering changes, and checking the register before it posts.
- **Employee changes.** New hires, terminations, raises, garnishments, and direct deposit updates, with documentation.
- **Deductions and benefits.** Health, 401(k), and other deductions set up and reconciled against carrier and plan statements.
- **Payroll taxes.** Deposits and quarterly and annual filings, whether in house or through a payroll provider, plus W-2s at year-end.
- **Reconciliation.** Tying payroll to the general ledger and the bank every period.
- **Questions.** Being the calm, private, accurate person employees ask about their paychecks.

## When to hire one

- Payroll is a side task for your bookkeeper or office manager, and mistakes are creeping in.
- You added employees in another state, hourly staff with overtime, or commissions and bonuses.
- You received a tax notice and are not sure why.
- You are changing payroll providers and need someone to run the conversion and parallel runs.

## Screening questions that work

1. **"How many employees, pay groups, and states did you process payroll for?"** Scale and complexity in one question.
2. **"Which payroll systems did you run end to end?"** Running a system is different from entering hours into it.
3. **"How did you reconcile payroll to the general ledger each period?"** Shows whether they connect payroll to accounting.
4. **"Tell us about a payroll error you caught before it went out."** Look for a review step, not luck.
5. **"How do you handle a garnishment order or a mid-period termination?"** Practical, detail-heavy, and common.
6. **"How do you protect employee pay data?"** Confidentiality is part of the job.

## Red flags

- Cannot describe how they check a payroll register before approving it.
- Has only entered hours, never handled tax filings or year-end.
- Talks casually about other employees' pay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a payroll specialist if I use a payroll provider?

Often, yes. A provider processes what it is given. Someone still has to collect accurate hours and changes, review each run, reconcile payroll to the books, and answer employee questions.

### Can a temp payroll specialist cover year-end?

Yes. Staff Financial Group places contract payroll and AP coverage, and it payrolls the temp as Employer of Record during the assignment.

### What is the difference between a payroll specialist and a payroll manager?

A payroll specialist runs and checks payroll. A payroll manager owns the whole function, sets controls, manages providers, and often leads a team. Staff Financial Group recruits both.
