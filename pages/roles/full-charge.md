## What "full-charge" means

"Full-charge" means the bookkeeper is in charge of the full cycle, not one slice of it. A full-charge bookkeeper typically:

- Records and categorizes all transactions, and runs or oversees AP and AR.
- Reconciles every bank, credit card, and loan account each month.
- Processes payroll, or manages the outside payroll provider and books the entries.
- Posts adjusting entries such as accruals, prepaid expenses, and depreciation, often with the CPA's guidance.
- Closes the month and produces the balance sheet and income statement.
- Prepares schedules and support for the CPA at tax time.
- Often handles sales tax filings and 1099s.

Compare that to an [AP or AR clerk](/roles/accounts-payable-and-receivable-clerks/), who owns transactions on one side, or a [staff accountant](/roles/staff-accountant/), who usually works inside a larger accounting team under a controller.

## When you need one

- You are a growing company with no one who owns the books day to day.
- The owner or office manager is doing the books at night.
- Your CPA is spending year-end cleaning up instead of advising.
- Your current bookkeeper is leaving, and the close cannot stop.

## What to screen for

The biggest risk is hiring someone who has supported a close but never owned one. Screening questions that sort that out:

1. **"Describe your last month-end close from start to finish. Which steps were yours?"**
2. **"What is the largest set of books you have owned alone? How many accounts, entities, and employees?"**
3. **"Tell us about a reconciliation that would not tie out. How did you find the difference?"**
4. **"How did you work with the outside CPA? What did you hand them at year-end?"**
5. **"Which systems did you use daily: QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage, or another?"**
6. **"Have you run payroll and filed sales tax yourself?"**

## Signs of a strong full-charge bookkeeper

- Closes on a predictable schedule and can tell you how many days it takes.
- Keeps a clean trail: support attached, notes on unusual entries.
- Explains the numbers in plain language to an owner who is not an accountant.
- Knows when a question is for the CPA and asks it early.

## Which hire type fits

- **[Direct Hire](/direct-hire-bookkeepers/)** is the most common choice, because continuity matters when one person owns the books.
- **[Temp-to-Hire](/temp-to-hire-bookkeepers/)** is a smart way to watch a full close cycle before you commit.
- **[Contract](/temp-contract-bookkeepers/)** to keep the close running between bookkeepers, or to clean up a backlog.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a bookkeeper and a full-charge bookkeeper?

A full-charge bookkeeper owns the whole cycle through the monthly close and financial statements. Other bookkeeping roles may focus on one part, like data entry, payables, or receivables.

### Does a full-charge bookkeeper replace my CPA?

No. A full-charge bookkeeper keeps the books current and accurate all year. Your CPA still handles tax returns, tax planning, and any review or audit work.

### Can a temp full-charge bookkeeper keep my close going while I hire?
