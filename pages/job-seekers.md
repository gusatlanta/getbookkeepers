## Get ready before you apply

- **Know your systems.** Employers ask which accounting systems you use every day. List them on your resume with how long you have used each one.
- **Know your numbers.** How many accounts you reconciled each month, how many invoices you processed each week, how many employees were on the payroll you ran. Specifics stand out.
- **Be ready to walk through a close.** Employers want to hear which steps you owned, start to finish.
- **Expect screening questions.** Many employers send questions with the application. Answer them fully. They often matter as much as the resume.
- **Record a video intro if you are invited.** It is a short way to show how you come across before the first interview.

For short, practical interview advice, see [One Minute Interview Tips](https://www.oneminuteinterviewtips.com/), including how to answer [salary expectations](https://www.oneminuteinterviewtips.com/questions/salary-expectations/) and interview tips for [accountants and finance](https://www.oneminuteinterviewtips.com/roles/accountants-and-finance/) roles.

## Know what the market pays

Staff Financial Group's [2026 Salary Guide](https://www.stafffinancial.com/salary-guide.html) shows what accounting people are asking to be paid. The median desired base salary was $70K for bookkeepers, $67K for AP, AR, and billing roles, and $70K for payroll and staff accountant roles, from 805 candidates between January and October 2026.
