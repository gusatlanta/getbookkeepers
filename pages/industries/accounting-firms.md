## What a bookkeeper does at an accounting firm

In industry, a bookkeeper keeps one company's books. At an accounting firm, a bookkeeper usually keeps many: monthly or quarterly write-up work for small business clients, getting their books ready for the tax preparers. Typical work includes:

- **Client write-up and reconciliations.** Categorizing transactions and reconciling bank and card accounts for a portfolio of clients, often in each client's own accounting file.
- **Client payroll.** Running payroll and payroll tax filings for clients who outsource it.
- **Sales tax and 1099s.** Filing sales tax returns on each client's schedule and preparing 1099s at year-end.
- **Tax-ready books.** Cleaning up and closing client books so the preparers can start returns without chasing missing items.
- **Client questions.** Requesting documents and explaining what is missing, in plain language.

## What makes it different

- **Many files, many deadlines.** A good firm bookkeeper tracks which clients are due for what, and switches between charts of accounts without mixing them up.
- **Peaks.** Work spikes before tax deadlines. Year-end payroll and 1099 work also lands in January, right as tax season starts.
- **Turnover.** In Staff Financial Group's [2026 Salary Guide](https://www.stafffinancial.com/salary-guide.html), BLS turnover data showed 55 departures per 100 jobs a year in professional services (which includes CPA firms), compared with 43 for all private employers. Planning ahead for open seats matters more here than in most industries.

## Skills to screen for

- **Volume and variety.** How many clients did they handle at once, and in which industries?
- **Systems.** Which accounting systems did they use daily, and have they worked in client files through an accountant's access? In the guide, QuickBooks was the most common system candidates named (60%).
- **Tax software exposure.** For bookkeepers who prepare workpapers for preparers, ask which tax software the firm used. Among tax candidates in the guide, the most named were UltraTax CS (24%), CCH Axcess (20%), GoSystem Tax RS (17%), and Lacerte (17%).
- **Payroll and sales tax filing.** Have they filed, not just entered data?
- **Communication.** Can they ask a client for documents clearly and politely?

## Screening questions to use

1. "How many client books did you keep at once, and how did you track deadlines?"
2. "Walk us through how you get a client's books ready for the tax preparer."
3. "Which payroll and sales tax filings did you handle yourself?"
4. "Tell us about a client file you cleaned up. What was wrong and how did you fix it?"

## Covering busy season

Many firms bring in [contract bookkeepers](/temp-contract-bookkeepers/) for the months before tax deadlines, so preparers are not doing write-up work. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a contract bookkeeper just for tax season?

Yes. Contract coverage for a defined period is a common request, and Staff Financial Group is the Employer of Record during the assignment.

### Should a firm bookkeeper know tax software?

It helps if they prepare workpapers for preparers. Put your firm's tax software in the screening questionnaire so you know who has used it.

### Is a staff accountant a better fit than a bookkeeper for a CPA firm?

It depends on the work. Write-up, payroll, and sales tax work suits an experienced bookkeeper. If the job includes accrual adjustments, reviews, or work toward the CPA, look at a [staff accountant](/roles/staff-accountant/).
