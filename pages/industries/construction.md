## What makes construction bookkeeping different

A construction company's profit is earned one job at a time, over months, and billed in stages. That makes the books harder than in a business that sells and invoices the same day. A construction bookkeeper keeps costs and billing tied to each job so the owner knows which jobs are making money while they are still running.

## What the job usually covers

- **Job costing.** Coding every bill, payroll hour, and material purchase to the right job and cost code, and comparing actual cost to budget.
- **Pay applications.** Preparing progress billings, often on the AIA G702 and G703 forms (the application and the continuation sheet), based on work completed.
- **Retainage.** Tracking amounts customers hold back until the job is complete, and amounts the company holds back from subcontractors.
- **Lien waivers.** Collecting waivers from subcontractors and suppliers before paying them, and providing them to customers when paid.
- **Work-in-progress (WIP) schedule.** For companies that recognize revenue over time, comparing billings to costs and estimated completion on each job to show overbillings and underbillings.
- **Payroll.** Field payroll with multiple jobs, job classifications, and sometimes union rules. On public jobs covered by prevailing wage laws, such as federally funded construction under the Davis-Bacon Act, contractors file certified payroll reports.
- **Subcontractor records.** Insurance certificates and W-9s, and 1099s at year-end.

## Skills to screen for

- **Job costing in your system.** Construction companies often run construction-specific accounting software or job costing add-ons. Put yours in the screening questionnaire.
- **Pay apps and retainage.** Ask them to explain how retainage shows up on a pay application.
- **WIP.** Ask whether they prepared or only supported the WIP schedule.
- **Field payroll.** Ask about multi-job time entry and, if you do public work, certified payroll.

## Screening questions to use

1. "Walk us through a monthly pay application, including retainage."
2. "How did you make sure every bill was coded to the right job and cost code?"
3. "What does an underbilling on a WIP schedule tell you?"
4. "How did you handle lien waivers before releasing a subcontractor payment?"

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a general bookkeeper learn construction accounting?

Yes, but job costing, pay applications, and WIP take time to learn. If you need someone productive right away, make construction experience a screening requirement.

### Is Temp-to-Hire a good fit for a contractor's first in-house bookkeeper?

Often, yes. Staff Financial Group's Cumming page notes Temp-to-Hire suits owners who want to see how someone works before committing.

### Do you recruit payroll specialists for field crews?

Yes. Staff Financial Group recruits [payroll specialists](/roles/payroll-specialist/), and multi-job or certified payroll experience can go into your screening questions.
