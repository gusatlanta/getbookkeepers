## What makes real estate bookkeeping different

Property owners, managers, and developers often keep a separate entity and bank account for each property. Rent comes in from many tenants, bills come in from many vendors, and owners expect a statement every month. A bookkeeper has to keep every transaction on the right property and every tenant balance current.

## What the job usually covers

- **Tenant ledgers and rent collection.** Posting rent and charges, applying payments, and following up on late tenants.
- **Security deposits.** Tracking deposits held for tenants, which are a liability and not income.
- **Property AP.** Coding maintenance and vendor bills to the right property.
- **Owner statements and distributions** for property management companies.
- **Bank reconciliations** across many property accounts.
- **Partnership books** for ownership entities, coordinated with the outside tax preparer.

## Skills to screen for

- **Property management software.** Put yours in the screening questionnaire.
- **Property count.** Ask how many properties or entities they kept books for.
- **Tenant receivables.** Ask how they handled late rent and charges.
- **Owner reporting.** Ask what went into the owner statements they prepared.

## Screening questions to use

1. "How did you keep vendor bills coded to the right property?"
2. "How do you record a tenant security deposit?"
3. "Walk us through a monthly owner statement."

## Frequently asked questions

### Is real estate bookkeeping different from construction bookkeeping?

Yes. Real estate is about properties, tenants, and owners. Construction is about jobs, costs, and progress billing. Developers often need both; see the [construction guide](/industries/construction/).
