## What makes technology and saas bookkeeping different

A subscription company often collects a full year of subscription payments on day one but earns that revenue month by month. The unearned part sits on the balance sheet as deferred revenue. Under U.S. GAAP (ASC 606), revenue follows what the customer receives, not when the cash arrives. A bookkeeper who books every invoice straight to revenue makes the income statement wrong.

## What the job usually covers

- **Subscription billing.** Invoicing from a billing system, handling upgrades, renewals, and cancellations.
- **Deferred revenue schedules.** Releasing revenue each month as it is earned.
- **Payments reconciliations.** Matching payment processor payouts to invoices and bank deposits.
- **Expense management.** Reviewing employee card spend and vendor bills for software and cloud services.
- **Audit readiness.** Keeping support for every balance so an outside audit or investor review goes smoothly.

## Skills to screen for

- **Deferred revenue.** Ask them to explain it in plain words.
- **Billing and payments systems.** List yours in the screening questionnaire.
- **Close speed.** Ask how many days their last close took and what they owned.
- **Audit.** Ask whether they prepared audit support.

## Screening questions to use

1. "A customer pays for a 12-month subscription up front. How do you record it?"
2. "How did you reconcile payment processor payouts to the bank?"
3. "What did you prepare for the auditors or investors at year-end?"

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a startup need a bookkeeper or a staff accountant?

Early on, a strong full-charge bookkeeper with subscription experience often covers it. As the close and reporting grow, a staff accountant becomes the better fit.

### Can we start with a contract bookkeeper before fundraising diligence?

Yes. Temp and Contract help can clean up and document the books, then you can hire the long-term person through Direct Hire.
