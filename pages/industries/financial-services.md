## What makes financial services bookkeeping different

Financial services firms are often regulated and audited, and they hold money or records on behalf of others. That makes reconciliations, documentation, and separation of duties more important than in most businesses. A bookkeeper here needs to be precise and comfortable working inside written procedures.

## What the job usually covers

- **Account reconciliations.** Many bank and clearing accounts, reconciled on a schedule with support for every reconciling item.
- **Commissions.** Calculating or recording producer and agent commissions.
- **AP with approvals** that follow written controls.
- **Audit support.** Pulling documentation for internal and external auditors.
- **Multi-location reporting** for firms with branches or agencies.

## Skills to screen for

- **Reconciliation volume.** Ask how many accounts they reconciled each month.
- **Controls.** Ask about approval steps and separation of duties at their last job.
- **Audit.** Ask what they pulled for auditors.
- **Commissions.** If you pay commissions, ask how they were calculated and checked.

## Screening questions to use

1. "How did you handle a reconciling item that was more than 60 days old?"
2. "Describe the approval steps for a payment at your last job."
3. "What documentation did you prepare for an audit?"

## Frequently asked questions

### Do financial services bookkeepers need a license?

Bookkeeping jobs usually do not, but your firm's own rules may require background checks. Put any requirement in your screening questions.

### Can a contract bookkeeper help with a reconciliation backlog?

Yes. Temp and Contract help is a common way to catch up before an audit.
