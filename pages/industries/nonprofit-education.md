## What makes nonprofit and education bookkeeping different

A nonprofit's donors and grantors often restrict how their money can be spent. Under U.S. GAAP, nonprofit financial statements separate net assets with donor restrictions from net assets without them, and report expenses by function, such as program services, management and general, and fundraising. A bookkeeper has to code every gift and every expense with that in mind.

## What the job usually covers

- **Recording gifts and grants** with any restrictions, and releasing restrictions when the money is used as intended.
- **Grant tracking.** Charging costs to the right grant and supporting grant reports.
- **Functional expense coding** for program, management and general, and fundraising.
- **Donor acknowledgments** and coordination with the development team.
- **Audit and Form 990 support**, working with the outside auditor and tax preparer.

## Skills to screen for

- **Restrictions.** Ask them to explain how a restricted gift is recorded and released.
- **Grants.** Ask how they charged costs to grants and what reports they supported.
- **Audit.** Ask what they prepared for the auditors.
- **Your software.** Put your accounting and donor systems in the questionnaire.

## Screening questions to use

1. "A donor gives for a specific program. How do you record it, and what happens when the money is spent?"
2. "How did you allocate shared costs across programs?"
3. "What did you prepare for your last audit?"

### Is nonprofit bookkeeping very different from business bookkeeping?

The basics are the same, but donor restrictions, grant tracking, and functional expense reporting take real experience. Make them screening requirements if you need someone productive right away.
