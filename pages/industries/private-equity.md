## What makes private equity bookkeeping different

At the firm or family office, the books cover many small entities: funds, the management company, holding companies, and sometimes personal entities for principals. Accuracy and discretion matter more than volume. At a portfolio company, the owner expects a monthly reporting package on a firm deadline, and lenders may require covenant reporting. A bookkeeper who is used to a loose close will struggle.

## What the job usually covers

- **Multi-entity books.** Separate ledgers and bank accounts for each entity, with intercompany entries kept in balance.
- **Capital calls and distributions.** Recording investor contributions and payouts, and supporting investor statements.
- **Tax coordination.** Organizing records for the outside tax preparer, including partnership entities.
- **Monthly reporting packages** at portfolio companies, on the sponsor's calendar.
- **Confidentiality.** Handling sensitive information about principals and deals.

## Skills to screen for

- **Entity count.** Ask how many entities they kept books for at once.
- **Partnership experience.** Ask whether they recorded capital calls and distributions.
- **Deadlines.** For portfolio companies, ask about their close calendar and reporting package.
- **Discretion.** Ask how they handled sensitive information.

## Screening questions to use

1. "How did you keep intercompany balances in agreement across entities?"
2. "Walk us through how you recorded a capital call."
3. "What went into the monthly package you sent to owners or lenders?"
