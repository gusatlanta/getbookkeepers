## What makes distribution and logistics bookkeeping different

Wholesalers, warehouses, and logistics companies move a lot of product on thin margins. The bookkeeping is not exotic, but the volume is high and mistakes multiply. A clerk who is fast but careless costs more in duplicate payments, missed customer deductions, and unapplied cash than a slower, careful one.

## What the job usually covers

- **High-volume AP.** Matching vendor invoices and freight bills to purchase orders and receipts, and catching duplicates.
- **Freight.** Auditing carrier bills and adding freight into the cost of inventory where the business does that.
- **Cash application.** Applying customer payments to the right invoices and researching short payments and deductions.
- **Credit and collections.** Following up on past-due accounts and keeping credit limits current.
- **Inventory reconciliations.** Tying warehouse counts to the general ledger.

## Skills to screen for

- **Volume.** Ask how many invoices or payments they handled a week and in what system.
- **Deductions.** Ask how they researched a customer short payment.
- **Freight.** Ask whether they audited carrier bills.
- **Language.** If your vendors or parent company need it, make language a screening requirement.

## Screening questions to use

1. "How many vendor invoices did you process in a typical week, and how did you catch duplicates?"
2. "A customer paid less than the invoice. What did you do next?"
3. "How did you match freight bills to shipments?"
