## Trust accounting: the part that cannot go wrong

Law firms often hold client money: retainers paid in advance, settlement funds, and money held for closings. That money belongs to the client until it is earned or paid out, and it is kept in a client trust account, separate from the firm's operating account. Small or short-term client funds are commonly held in a pooled IOLTA account (Interest on Lawyers' Trust Accounts). In Georgia, the rules for safekeeping client property and keeping trust account records are in Rule 1.15 of the Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct. The lawyers are responsible for compliance, and the bookkeeper does the daily work that keeps the firm compliant.

A legal bookkeeper should know these basics without being taught:

- **No commingling.** Firm money and client money stay separate. Earned fees move from trust to operating only when they are earned and properly documented.
- **Client ledgers.** Every client with money in trust has their own ledger, and no client's balance should ever go below zero.
- **Three-way reconciliation.** Each month, the trust bank statement, the trust account register, and the total of all client ledgers must agree.
- **Records.** Deposits, disbursements, and the reason for each must be documented and kept.

## Billing and receivables

Most firms bill by time. A legal bookkeeper often works with time entries, prepares or reviews client invoices, applies retainers from trust to invoices, and follows up on unpaid bills. Larger firms may also need partner-level billing reports and cost recovery.

## Skills to screen for

- **Trust accounting experience.** Ask them to explain a three-way reconciliation.
- **Legal billing or practice management software.** Put your system in the screening questionnaire.
- **Discretion.** Law firm bookkeepers see confidential client information every day.

## Screening questions to use

1. "Walk us through your monthly three-way trust reconciliation."
2. "A client's trust ledger would go negative if you paid a bill. What do you do?"
3. "How did you apply a retainer in trust to an invoice?"
4. "Which billing or practice management system did you use?"

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a bookkeeper with law firm experience?

For trust accounting, yes. It is the area where mistakes create the most risk, and an experienced legal bookkeeper already knows the routine.

### Can you run a confidential search for a law firm bookkeeper?

Yes. Staff Financial Group regularly runs confidential replacement searches, and candidate profiles are blinded until you are ready to meet someone.

### Is a legal bookkeeper the same as a paralegal?

No. A paralegal supports legal work. A legal bookkeeper handles the firm's books, billing, and trust accounts.
