## What makes manufacturing bookkeeping different

In a service business, a bill is an expense. In a manufacturer, much of what the company buys becomes inventory first and an expense only when the product ships. That means the bookkeeper's coding decides whether cost of goods sold, and so the gross margin, is right. Small errors in receiving or inventory show up as big swings at month-end.

## What the job usually covers

- **Three-way match.** Matching each vendor invoice to the purchase order and the receiving record before it is paid.
- **Inventory reconciliations.** Tying the inventory in the system to the general ledger and to cycle counts, and researching differences.
- **Cost of goods sold.** Recording the cost of what shipped, often from standard costs set in the ERP.
- **Freight and accruals.** Recording freight in and out, and accruing for goods received but not yet billed.
- **Shift payroll.** Hourly workers, overtime, and multiple shifts, often with a timekeeping system feeding payroll.

## Skills to screen for

- **Your ERP.** Put it in the screening questionnaire. Manufacturing systems differ a lot from small business software.
- **Three-way match and receiving.** Ask how they handled a receipt with no invoice, and an invoice with no receipt.
- **Inventory.** Ask whether they reconciled inventory to the ledger or only entered transactions.
- **Payroll volume.** For payroll jobs, ask about hourly, multi-shift workforces.

## Screening questions to use

1. "Walk us through what you check before paying a vendor invoice for raw materials."
2. "How did you find the cause of a difference between the inventory report and the general ledger?"
3. "What does a freight accrual do at month-end?"
